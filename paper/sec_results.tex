\paragraph{In-distribution gains are small and consistent.} On held-out WT pucks the expression-only logistic regression reaches 0.699 macro-F1, and every form of spatial context adds about one point: 0.711 for neighbourhood-augmented LR and for GraphSAGE, 0.712 for test-time smoothing (Table~\ref{tab:main}). The gain is small but systematic, positive on all 15 setting--test-puck combinations. A neighbourhood-only classifier reaches 0.636, so most of the stage signal is in the bead itself. Gains are similar on \emph{ob/ob} and larger on human folds, where the expression-only ceiling is lower (0.486) and neighbourhood features add 2.7 points (0.513). Neighbourhood size matters little (at most 0.008 across $k\in\{5,15,30\}$), and an MLP on the same features is 0.6 to 2.1 points below the LR in every setting (Tables~\ref{tab:germ4full},~\ref{tab:sweeps}).

\paragraph{The \emph{ob/ob} shift is small for this task.} Models trained on WT pucks perform as well on \emph{ob/ob} pucks as models trained on other \emph{ob/ob} pucks (0.713 vs.\ 0.717 expression only; 0.721 vs.\ 0.723 with neighbourhood features). The spatial gains carry over unchanged, and germ-cell homogeneity overlaps between conditions (0.49 to 0.57 vs.\ 0.53 to 0.56). A classifier trained to tell WT from \emph{ob/ob} beads inside the models' feature space performs at chance (AUC 0.499 own, 0.519 neighbourhood; Table~\ref{tab:shift}), so the condition is not a covariate shift in the space the models see, and the disruption reported by \citet{chen2021dissecting} does not register at this resolution.

\paragraph{Species shift is large and is dominated by expression.} Expression-only macro-F1 falls from 0.486 (human$\to$human) to 0.385 (WT$\to$human), and adding the \emph{ob/ob} pucks to training does not change it (0.385). Neighbourhood-augmented LR retains a gain under species shift (0.398, $+1.3$), as does test-time smoothing (0.401, $+1.6$). GraphSAGE does not: it is within 0.008 of neighbourhood-augmented LR in every in-distribution setting and becomes the worst model across species, at 0.347 ($-3.8$ vs.\ expression only, $-5.1$ vs.\ neighbourhood LR; seed-to-seed SD $\le 0.007$). Per stage (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}B), the loss is concentrated on round spermatids, the largest human class, whose F1 drops from 0.390 to 0.237 with GraphSAGE and to 0.376 with neighbourhood LR; with neighbourhood LR, spermatogonia and spermatocytes still gain about 3 points.

\paragraph{The neighbourhood representation is the less stable one.} How far each stage's centroid moves between training and test pucks, in units of its own within-class spread, separates the two feature spaces (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}A). Both are nearly stationary within a species (0.12 own, 0.19 neighbourhood) and under the \emph{ob/ob} condition (0.09, 0.16), and both move across species (0.35, 0.46). In all 24 stage-by-puck comparisons the neighbourhood centroid moves further than the bead's own, by factors of 1.06 to 3.30: a fixed average inherits that drift once, whereas a model learning a function of the neighbourhood distribution is fitted to it. Round spermatids show both the largest cross-species neighbourhood displacement (0.53) and the largest GraphSAGE loss (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}B,C), which with four stages we read as consistent with the mechanism rather than evidence for it.

\paragraph{Secondary observations.} The gain from context does not track the target puck's neighbourhood homogeneity: the largest gains occur on the human pucks, which have the lowest (0.38 to 0.40). It tracks instead how informative the neighbourhood is relative to the bead, a within-sample association rather than a usable predictor, since both come from the same labels (Fig.~\ref{fig:perclass}C). Test-time smoothing peaks at $\alpha=0.5$ in every setting (Fig.~\ref{fig:perclass}D). At the bead level the within-species gain sits in the lowest-UMI tertile, and context lowers accuracy for beads with heterogeneous neighbourhoods (Table~\ref{tab:strat}).
